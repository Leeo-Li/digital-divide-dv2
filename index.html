Who Is Left Behind in Australia's Digital Life?

Digital inclusion is not only about internet access. In the Australian Digital Inclusion Index (ADII), a higher score out of 100 means better access, affordability and digital ability. This story explores who is left behind through four dimensions: the national picture, place-based gaps, demographic differences and risk drivers.

Summary

The divide is not one problem

Australia's digital divide is strongest where affordability pressure, lower digital ability and social disadvantage overlap. Access has improved, but digital inclusion still depends on income, age, education, housing and location.

Main barrier Affordability is the clearest warning sign.

It is the weakest national dimension and the clearest divider between low-income and high-income households.

Most at-risk groups Risk concentrates among low-income, older and lower-education groups.

These groups sit below national benchmarks for different reasons: cost pressure affects some groups, while ability barriers affect others.

Key takeaway A single national score hides real-world gaps.

Digital inclusion looks stronger at the national level than it does for many specific groups. Location, income, age, education and social disadvantage reveal who is more likely to be left behind.
